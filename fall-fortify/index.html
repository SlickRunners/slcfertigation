Service One

Aeration with Compost & Humate

Utah's clay-heavy soil compacts hard over a summer of foot traffic and heat. Core aeration pulls real plugs of soil out of your lawn, and we follow it immediately with a compost and humate topdress that works its way down into those open holes — feeding the soil biology your grass depends on, not just the grass itself.

  • ✓
    Relieves compaction Opens channels for air, water, and roots to actually reach the soil instead of running off a hardened surface.
  • ✓
    Feeds soil biology Compost and humate introduce organic matter and microbial activity that synthetic fertilizer alone can't replace.
  • ✓
    Improves water efficiency Aerated, amended soil holds moisture longer, so your existing watering schedule goes further.
🌾

Why fall is the right time

Cool soil temperatures and reliable moisture let roots recover fast after aeration, and grass has months to establish before summer stress returns.

02
Service Two

Grass Overseeding

Thin patches, bare spots, and sparse coverage don't fix themselves — they need new seed making contact with soil, at the right time of year. We overseed right after aeration, while those open holes are still exposed, so new seed lands exactly where it can germinate instead of sitting on top of thatch.

  • ✓
    Thickens the whole lawn New grass fills in thin and bare areas, crowding out weeds before they get a chance to establish next spring.
  • ✓
    Seed-to-soil contact Timed right after aeration so seed falls into open cores instead of resting on the surface where it dries out.
  • ✓
    Builds long-term density A thicker stand of grass shades out weed seeds and holds up better against next summer's heat and traffic.
🌱

Why fall is the right time

Warm soil and cooling air are ideal for cool-season grass germination, with far less weed competition than a spring overseed.

7–14 Days to germinate
1 Season to establish
03
Service Three

Sprinkler Blowout

Water left in your irrigation lines over a Utah winter freezes, expands, and cracks pipes, valves, and sprinkler heads — damage you don't discover until you turn the system back on in spring. A compressed-air blowout clears every line completely before the first hard freeze hits.

  • ✓
    Prevents freeze damage Clears trapped water from pipes, valves, and heads so nothing is left to freeze and crack over winter.
  • ✓
    Avoids spring repair costs A proper blowout is a fraction of the cost of replacing burst pipes or heads discovered next April.
  • ✓
    System ready on day one Start next season with a fully functional system instead of troubleshooting leaks before your first watering.
❄️

Why fall is the right time

Once nighttime temperatures start dropping toward freezing, every day you wait is a day of risk to pipes and heads that are cheap to protect and expensive to replace.

80–100 PSI compressed air
